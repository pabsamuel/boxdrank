## The message

Short, no product, no link, one question. The goal is an answer, not a demo.

> Hi — I saw your post about monday not notifying you when an automation stops.
>
> I'm looking into that problem and I'd rather hear it from someone it actually
> happened to than guess.
>
> When one of yours stopped, how did you find out, and how long had it been
> broken?
>
> Not selling anything, just trying to understand whether this is annoying or
> actually expensive.

That is the whole message. **No link. No product name. No "would you use a tool
that…"** — that question gets a polite yes from everyone and means nothing.

### The follow-up, only if they reply

> That's useful, thank you. One more: what do you do about it today — is there
> a routine, or do you just find out when someone complains?
>
> And if something watched for it and emailed you, is that a "nice to have" or
> would you actually put it on the company card? Rough number is fine.

**The price question has to be asked out loud.** "Would you pay?" gets a yes.
"What would you put on the card, roughly?" gets a number or an awkward pause, and
the awkward pause is the answer.

## Counting it

Send **10**. Not 30 — these are targeted messages to specific people, not a
campaign.

| Outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| ≥4 reply, ≥2 name a number ≥ $10/month | Continue. Wire up hosting and submit. |
| ≥4 reply, nobody names a number | Real problem, no budget. That is a free tool, not a business. Stop. |
| ≤2 reply | Either the message is wrong or the pain is not top-of-mind. Rewrite once, send 10 more, then stop. |
| "It happened, we just check manually" with no irritation | **The killer answer.** Stop. |

### The answer that kills it

If people say the automation broke and **nobody actually cared** — the work got
done another way, someone noticed eventually, no money was lost — then this is a
problem people tolerate, and tolerated problems do not get expensed. That is the
same answer that killed the Board Schema Auditor, and it will be obvious within
the first three replies.

### The answer that makes it real

Anything with a cost attached. *"We invoiced a client two weeks late."* *"The
handover Slack messages stopped and nobody picked up the tickets."* *"We found
out when the customer complained."* A problem with a number attached is a problem
someone buys a fix for.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not show the app first.** A demo turns the conversation into feedback on
  the UI instead of evidence about the problem.
- **Do not ask "would you use this".** Everyone says yes. It has predicted
  nothing, twice.
- **Do not send 30.** Ten targeted messages to people with the problem beats
  thirty to a list, and if ten pre-qualified people do not care, thirty will not
  either.
- **Do not count a reply as validation.** Only a number counts.
