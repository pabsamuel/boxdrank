# PROMPT: DM handling and launch copy

## Part A — DM triage (do NOT automate the replies themselves)

Your DMs are where subscribers come from. People pay for *you*, and a templated reply is
obvious and fatal. Use Claude to build your own reusable answers **once**, then send them
yourself, edited each time.

```
Here are the 15 most common questions I get in DMs:
[paste them]

For each, draft a reply that:
- is under 40 words
- answers in Arabic, with any English terms left in English
- sounds like one professional talking to another, never like a brand
- ends by asking them one question back (this is how a DM becomes a conversation
  and a conversation becomes a subscriber)
- never begs, never over-explains, never uses exclamation marks

Then tell me which 3 of these questions are so common they should just become posts.
```

**The rule:** anyone who gets a real conversation from you is worth 100 passive followers.
Never let Claude write the actual live reply. Use it to prepare, not to speak.

## Part B — The waitlist launch sequence

Run this two weeks before opening seats. See `strategy/OFFER.md` for the 5-day window model.

```
Read langpage/strategy/OFFER.md and langpage/strategy/MARKET.md.

I'm opening 40 seats for "The Room" ($39/month) in two weeks, to a waitlist of [N] people.

Here are real answers from my waitlist to the question "what happens in the moment
you freeze in English?":
[paste 10-20 verbatim answers]

Produce:

1. A sales page (Arabic, with English section headers) built almost entirely from their
   own words. Structure: the moment of freezing → why school English didn't fix it →
   what the 4 weeks look like concretely → what's included with exact numbers
   (3 voice notes/week, 24h weekday reply, weekly 45-min call) → who this is NOT for →
   price, cap, refund policy → FAQ.

2. A 5-email launch sequence for the 5-day window: day 1 open, day 2 objection
   (no time), day 3 proof/story, day 4 objection (I'm too shy to speak), day 5 closing
   in 6 hours. Under 200 words each.

3. 5 Instagram story frames per day of the window, showing real seat counts.

Rules:
- No fake scarcity. The 40-seat cap is real; use the true remaining number.
- No invented testimonials, no invented results, no "students" I don't have.
- No income or fluency guarantees. Promise the process, not the outcome.
- "Who this is NOT for" must genuinely exclude beginners and people wanting passive study.
```

**Why the "not for you" section matters:** it is usually the highest-converting part of a page
aimed at professionals. It signals you're selecting, not begging.
